Contrail API Terms of Service

Effective Date: November 2026 • Mandatory acceptable use policy and upstream attribution requirements.

Article 1

Strictly Non-Commercial Use Only

Contrail API is provided exclusively for non-commercial purposes, including individual study, academic research, open-source utilities, and personal hobby projects. You may not resell, sub-license, repackage, or incorporate this data into any commercial, revenue-generating, subscription-based, or ad-monetized product or service. Commercial applications are not permitted; prospective commercial users must apply for direct licenses from the upstream data providers.

Article 2

Mandatory Attribution Retention

When publicly displaying, redistributing, or forwarding data obtained through this API, you must preserve and display the upstream source attribution (as returned in the attribution envelope of each response).

Article 3

Prohibition of Substitute Database Scraping

Automated batch scraping for the purpose of creating a competing public telemetry mirror or substitute database is strictly prohibited.

Article 4

Unofficial Data & Non-Navigation Disclaimer

All flight tracking data is unofficial and provided on an "as-is" basis without warranties of any kind. Telemetry may be delayed, intermittent, or subject to sensor anomalies. This service must never be used for actual aircraft navigation, air traffic control, or safety-of-life decisions.

Article 5

Rate Limiting & Revocation Rights

Contrail reserves the right to apply rate limiting, suspend accounts, or revoke API keys at any time without prior notice in the event of terms violations, commercial abuse, or abusive traffic patterns.

Article 6

Upstream Intellectual Property & Academic Citation

All live state vectors, route tables, and airport identifiers remain the property of their respective providers. When citing telemetry derived from The OpenSky Network in academic literature, you must include the following reference:

Matthias Schäfer, Martin Strohmeier, Vincent Lenders, Ivan Martinovic and Matthias Wilhelm. "Bringing Up OpenSky: A Large-scale ADS-B Sensor Network for Research". In Proceedings of the 13th IEEE/ACM International Symposium on Information Processing in Sensor Networks (IPSN), pages 83-94, April 2014.
